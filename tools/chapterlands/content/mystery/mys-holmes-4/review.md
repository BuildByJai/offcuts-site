## The Walking Stick (Chapter 1) · PUZZLE

1. *what* — What was engraved on the visitor’s walking stick?
   - **“To James Mortimer, M.R.C.S., from his friends of the C.C.H.”**
   - “Property of Sherlock Holmes”
   - “Baskerville Hall, 1884”
   - Evidence (chapter 1): "“To James Mortimer, M.R.C.S., from his friends of the C.C.H.,” was engraved upon it, with the date “1884.”"
   - Hint: Look again at the start, when Watson picks up the stick.

2. *why* — How did Holmes know the stick’s owner had a dog?
   - **The dog’s teeth marks were on the stick**
   - There were dog hairs on it
   - The dog was in the room
   - Evidence (chapter 1): "Being a heavy stick the dog has held it tightly by the middle, and the marks of his teeth are very plainly visible."
   - Hint: Look again at what Holmes sees in the middle of the stick.

3. *think* — Watson guessed an old, grave family doctor; Holmes saw a young man under thirty. What does this show?
   - **Follow the clues, not your first guess**
   - Watson is always right
   - Sticks can’t tell you anything
   - Evidence (chapter 1): "So your grave, middle-aged family practitioner vanishes into thin air, my dear Watson, and there emerges a young fellow under thirty, amiable, unambitious, absentminded, and the possessor of a favourite dog"
   - Hint: Look again at how Holmes corrects Watson.

## The Curse (Chapter 2, 3) · COLLECT

1. *what* — What did Dr. Mortimer see on the ground near Sir Charles?
   - **The footprints of a gigantic hound**
   - A broken walking stick
   - A lantern
   - Evidence (chapter 2): "Holmes, they were the footprints of a gigantic hound!”"
   - Hint: Look again at the very end of the chapter.

2. *why* — Why would Sir Charles never go out on the moor at night?
   - **He had taken the old family legend to heart**
   - He couldn’t see in the dark
   - The moor was closed at night
   - Evidence (chapter 2): "He had taken this legend which I have read you exceedingly to heart—so much so that, although he would walk in his own grounds, nothing would induce him to go out upon the moor at night."
   - Hint: Look again at what Dr. Mortimer says about Sir Charles.

3. *think* — Dr. Mortimer says Sir Charles’s constant worry was harming his health. What does this show?
   - **Fear itself can make someone ill**
   - Legends are always true
   - Sir Charles was never worried
   - Evidence (chapter 2): "His heart was, I knew, affected, and the constant anxiety in which he lived, however chimerical the cause of it might be, was evidently having a serious effect upon his health."
   - Hint: Look again at what the doctor says about Sir Charles’s heart.
