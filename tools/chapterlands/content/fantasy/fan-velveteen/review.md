# Review — The Velveteen Rabbit

Level 1 · fantasy · readable in-game: yes

Right answer in **bold**. Evidence is the supporting sentence from the text (never shown to players).

## The Nursery (Part 1) · COLLECT

1. *what* — Where was the Velveteen Rabbit on Christmas morning?
   - **In the top of the Boy's stocking**
   - Under the Christmas tree
   - In a box on the table
   - Evidence (part 1): "On Christmas morning, when he sat wedged in the top of the Boy's stocking, with a sprig of holly between his paws, the effect was charming."
   - Hint: Look again at the very first part, on Christmas morning.

2. *why* — Why did the Rabbit start sleeping in the Boy's bed?
   - **The Boy couldn't find his china dog, so Nana gave him the Rabbit**
   - The Rabbit was the Boy's newest present
   - The Skin Horse asked Nana to put him there
   - Evidence (part 1): "One evening, when the Boy was going to bed, he couldn't find the china dog that always slept with him."
   - Hint: Look again at the part where Nana is in a hurry at bedtime.

3. *think* — What does the Skin Horse's talk about Real show?
   - **Being loved matters more than how you look**
   - The most expensive toys are the most Real
   - Becoming Real happens all at once, like being wound up
   - Evidence (part 1): "But these things don't matter at all, because once you are Real you can't be ugly, except to people who don't understand."
   - Hint: Look again at what the Skin Horse says about hair being loved off and getting shabby.

## The Garden (Part 2) · CHASE

1. *what* — Where did the Boy leave the Rabbit when he played in the wood?
   - **In a little nest in the bracken**
   - Up on the branch of a tree
   - In the wheelbarrow by the gate
   - Evidence (part 2): "he always made the Rabbit a little nest somewhere among the bracken, where he would be quite cosy, for he was a kind-hearted little boy and he liked Bunny to be comfortable."
   - Hint: Look again at the part about the wood near the house, in the long June evenings.

2. *why* — Why did the Rabbit keep saying he didn't want to play with the wild rabbits?
   - **He didn't want them to see he couldn't hop like them**
   - He was too tired after the Boy's games
   - He was scared of the ants
   - Evidence (part 2): "That was a dreadful question, for the Velveteen Rabbit had no hind legs at all!"
   - Hint: Look again at the part where the wild rabbit asks if he can hop on his hind legs.

3. *think* — The wild rabbit said, "He isn't real!" Why did the little Rabbit still say "I am Real!"?
   - **Because the Boy loved him and said he was Real**
   - Because he could jump higher than anything
   - Because he was furry and brand-new
   - Evidence (part 2): ""I am Real!" said the little Rabbit. "I am Real! The Boy said so!""
   - Hint: Look again at what the little Rabbit says, just before he nearly begins to cry.

## The Fairy (Part 3) · CLIMB

1. *what* — What grew where the little Rabbit's tear fell?
   - **A mysterious flower**
   - A raspberry cane
   - A tiny tree
   - Evidence (part 3): "For where the tear had fallen a flower grew out of the ground, a mysterious flower, not at all like any that grew in the garden."
   - Hint: Look again at the part at the end of the garden, at night, after the tear falls.

2. *why* — Why did the old Bunny have to go away with the old picture-books?
   - **The doctor said he was full of germs from the Boy's illness**
   - The Boy didn't love him any more
   - Nana wanted to give him to another child
   - Evidence (part 3): ""That?" said the doctor. "Why, it's a mass of scarlet fever germs!"
   - Hint: Look again at what the doctor says when Nana asks about the old Bunny.

3. *think* — The Fairy said, "You were Real to the Boy… Now you shall be Real to every one." What does this show?
   - **The Boy's love had made him Real, and now everyone would see it**
   - He was never Real at all until the Fairy came
   - Only fairies can make toys Real
   - Evidence (part 3): ""You were Real to the Boy," the Fairy said, "because he loved you. Now you shall be Real to every one.""
   - Hint: Look again at what the Fairy says when the Rabbit asks, "Wasn't I Real before?"

## Vault — the whole book

1. Who was the only one kind to the Velveteen Rabbit in the nursery at first?
   - **The Skin Horse**
   - Timothy, the wooden lion
   - The model boat
   - Evidence (part 1): "Between them all the poor little Rabbit was made to feel himself very insignificant and commonplace, and the only person who was kind to him at all was the Skin Horse."
   - Hint: Think back to the very start, in the nursery.

2. Who first said out loud that the Rabbit was REAL?
   - **The Boy**
   - Nana
   - The wild rabbits
   - Evidence (part 1): ""Give me my Bunny!" he said. "You mustn't say that. He isn't a toy. He's REAL!""
   - Hint: Think about the night the Rabbit was left out on the lawn.

3. What happened to the Rabbit while the Boy loved him so hard?
   - **He grew old and shabby, and his whiskers were loved off**
   - He grew shinier and newer every day
   - He grew clockwork inside him
   - Evidence (part 3): "He loved him so hard that he loved all his whiskers off, and the pink lining to his ears turned grey, and his brown spots faded."
   - Hint: The Skin Horse said this would happen to anyone who becomes Real.

4. What did the wild rabbits have that the Velveteen Rabbit longed for?
   - **Hind legs to hop and dance with**
   - Clockwork keys
   - Ears lined with pink sateen
   - Evidence (part 2): "But all the while he was longing to dance, for a funny new tickly feeling ran through him, and he felt he would give anything in the world to be able to jump about like these rabbits did."
   - Hint: Think about the summer evening in the bracken.

5. What does the nursery magic Fairy do for playthings that children have loved?
   - **She takes them away and turns them into Real**
   - She mends them so they look new again
   - She gives them to other children
   - Evidence (part 3): "When they are old and worn out and the children don't need them any more, then I come and take them away with me and turn them into Real."
   - Hint: Think about what the Fairy says when she first speaks to the Rabbit.

6. In the spring, why did one of the rabbits in the wood look familiar to the Boy?
   - **He had spots showing through his fur, like the old Bunny**
   - He was wearing the Boy's scarf
   - He came when the Boy called his name
   - Evidence (part 3): "One of them was brown all over, but the other had strange markings under his fur, as though long ago he had been spotted, and the spots still showed through."
   - Hint: Think about the very last part of the story.

7. What does the whole story say makes you Real?
   - **Being truly loved for a long, long time**
   - Being expensive and clever
   - Never getting old or worn out
   - Evidence (part 1): "When a child loves you for a long, long time, not just to play with, but REALLY loves you, then you become Real."
   - Hint: Think about the Skin Horse — and about the Boy.

- Vault puzzle — The toy cupboard (order): First at the top, last at the bottom.
  - Order: Christmas morning, in the top of the Boy's stocking → The Skin Horse explains what Real means → The Boy says: "He isn't a toy. He's REAL!" → The wild rabbits say he isn't real → A flower grows where his tear fell, and the Fairy steps out
- Vault puzzle — The Skin Horse's corner (choice): What really made the little Rabbit Real?
  - Answer: The Boy loved him for a long, long time
