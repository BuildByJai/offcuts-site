## The Printing Shop (Part 1) · ARCADE

1. *what* — What bold project did Franklin begin?
   - **Arriving at moral perfection**
   - Building a printing press
   - Sailing to London
   - Evidence (part 1): "It was about this time I conceived the bold and arduous project of arriving at moral perfection."
   - Hint: Look again at the very first line.

2. *why* — Why was it harder than he expected?
   - **While he guarded against one fault, another surprised him**
   - He had no time at all
   - Nobody would help him
   - Evidence (part 1): "While my care was employed in guarding against one fault, I was often surprised by another; habit took the advantage of inattention; inclination was sometimes too strong for reason."
   - Hint: Look again at what went wrong.

3. *think* — Franklin says good habits must be “acquired and established”. What does he mean?
   - **Knowing what is right is not enough — you have to practise it**
   - You can buy good habits
   - Habits never change
   - Evidence (part 1): "and that the contrary habits must be broken, and good ones acquired and established, before we can have any dependence on a steady, uniform rectitude of conduct."
   - Hint: Look again at what he concluded.

## The 13 Virtues (Part 2) · BUILD

1. *what* — How long did Franklin give to each virtue?
   - **A week’s strict attention**
   - One day
   - A whole year
   - Evidence (part 2): "I determined to give a week’s strict attention to each of the virtues successively."
   - Hint: Look again at the plan.

2. *why* — Why did he put Temperance first?
   - **It gives a cool, clear head for the rest**
   - It was the easiest to spell
   - His friend told him to
   - Evidence (part 2): "Temperance first, as it tends to procure that coolness and clearness of head, which is so necessary where constant vigilance was to be kept up"
   - Hint: Look again at his reasons for the order.

3. *think* — Franklin compares his plan to weeding a garden one bed at a time. Why?
   - **Trying to fix everything at once is too much**
   - He loved gardening
   - Weeds are like virtues
   - Evidence (part 2): "And like him who, having a garden to weed, does not attempt to eradicate all the bad herbs at once, which would exceed his reach and his strength, but works on one of the beds at a time"
   - Hint: Think about the one-at-a-time plan.

## The Little Book (Part 3) · PUZZLE

1. *what* — Which virtue gave Franklin the most trouble?
   - **Order**
   - Temperance
   - Silence
   - Evidence (part 3): "My scheme of _Order_ gave me the most trouble"
   - Hint: Look again at what he found hardest.

2. *why* — Why did Franklin add Humility to his list?
   - **A Quaker friend told him he was thought proud**
   - It was on every list
   - He had run out of ideas
   - Evidence (part 3): "My list of virtues contained at first but twelve; but a Quaker friend having kindly informed me that I was generally thought proud"
   - Hint: Look again at the thirteenth virtue.

3. *think* — The man said, “I think I like a speckled ax best.” What is Franklin warning about?
   - **Giving up and pretending your faults don’t matter**
   - Buying cheap tools
   - Working too hard
   - Evidence (part 3): "have given up the struggle, and concluded that “a speckled ax was best”"
   - Hint: Look again at the story of the smith.

## Vault — the whole book

1. What did Franklin mark in his little book for every fault?
   - **A little black spot**
   - A gold star
   - A tick
   - Evidence (part 2): "I might mark, by a little black spot, every fault I found upon examination to have been committed respecting that virtue upon that day."
   - Hint: Think about the pages of the little book.

2. What question did Franklin ask himself each morning?
   - **What good shall I do this day?**
   - What shall I eat today?
   - Who shall I meet today?
   - Evidence (part 2): "What good shall I do this day?"
   - Hint: Think about his daily timetable.

3. How long was one complete course through all the virtues?
   - **Thirteen weeks**
   - Thirteen days
   - Thirteen years
   - Evidence (part 2): "Proceeding thus to the last, I could go through a course complete in thirteen weeks, and four courses in a year."
   - Hint: Think about one virtue a week.

4. Did Franklin become perfect?
   - **No — but he became a better and a happier man**
   - Yes, completely
   - He gave up after a week
   - Evidence (part 3): "But, on the whole, though I never arrived at the perfection I had been so ambitious of obtaining, but fell far short of it, yet I was, by the endeavour, a better and a happier man than I otherwise should have been if I had not attempted it"
   - Hint: Think about how it ended.

5. Why does Franklin say he might be “proud of my humility”?
   - **Pride is so hard to beat it sneaks back in**
   - He was very humble
   - Humility was easy
   - Evidence (part 3): "In reality, there is, perhaps, no one of our natural passions so hard to subdue as _pride_."
   - Hint: Think about the last virtue.

6. What did Franklin always carry with him, even abroad?
   - **His little book**
   - His printing press
   - His kite
   - Evidence (part 3): "but I always carried my little book with me."
   - Hint: Think about his travels.
